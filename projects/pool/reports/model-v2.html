Model v2 — Blending Our Data

We tried to fold squad value, form, H2H and injuries into the match model. Then we tested whether it actually helps. Here is what we found and what we shipped.

1. The goal

The v1 forecast used Elo ratings only. The plan for v2 was to blend in everything else we collected: squad market value, recent form, head-to-head records, and injuries. Before trusting any of it, we measured each signal against reality.

2. The test (and the surprise)

We rebuilt Elo and rolling form match-by-match across history, then predicted a held-out window of 3,440 real internationals (Jan 2023 – May 2026) the model had never updated on. Scoring metric: Ranked Probability Score (RPS), lower is better.

ModelRPSLog-lossAccuracy
Elo only (home adv 100)0.16840.86660.3%
Best after grid search (home adv 80)0.16830.86660.5%
Elo + recent form0.16830.86660.5%
Key finding: adding recent form gave zero improvement (best form weight = 0). The reason is structural: Elo is itself a running update after every match, so it already contains recent form. Bolting form on again is double-counting. By the same logic head-to-head adds little — and it is sparse (many 2026 pairings have never met). An RPS of ~0.168 with 60% accuracy is already a solid, market-comparable international model.

3. What we actually shipped

So instead of blending things Elo already knows, v2 adds only the two signals Elo genuinely cannot see, as small, bounded priors on top of the calibrated rating:

  • Injury / availability penalty. Elo has no idea a key player is hurt. We subtract Elo points scaled by the market value of unavailable squad members (a fitness doubt counts half), capped at −45.
  • Squad-value talent prior. When results lag the squad, market value is a forward-looking strength hint. We nudge by up to ±45 Elo from the team's value relative to the field.
  • Form and H2H: deliberately excluded, with evidence, rather than included on faith.
  • Home advantage recalibrated from 100 to 80 for prediction.

Elo stays the backbone; these priors only perturb it. Full breakdown is in data/adjusted_strength.json.

European heavyweights with deep, expensive squads rise; teams whose Elo outruns their current talent or who lost the host bump (Colombia, Mexico) and the injury-hit Dutch ease back. The shifts are modest by design: Elo is right most of the time, and we only correct where it is blind.

6. Honest limitations & what is next

  • The value and injury priors are reasoned, not backtested — we only have today's squad values, so they cannot be validated historically the way form was. Weights are kept small for that reason.
  • Independent-Poisson slightly under-counts draws → add the Dixon-Coles correction next.
  • To truly train on value/injuries we would need a historical panel of squad values per match — a real data project.
  • Next validation step: backtest the full pipeline's tournament odds against the betting market and the 2018/2022 results.
