Model bake-off & agent ensemble

We pitted six prediction algorithms against each other on held-out history, combined them, and pushed the winner into the 2026 forecast. Here is the whole run.

Set-up

Every model is an "agent" that turns a fixture into win/draw/loss probabilities. We score them on real matches the model never trained on, using a strict time split and the football-standard Ranked Probability Score (RPS, lower is better) plus log-loss and accuracy.

TRAIN  < 2020-01-01     (fit the learning models)
VAL    2020 .. 2023      (tune ensemble weights, calibration)
TEST   2023 .. 2026      (3,431 matches, untouched until the end)

What we learned

  • Machine learning on Elo-derived features does not beat Elo. The gradient-boosted and logistic models trained on form, goals and Elo just re-learn what Elo already says, so they land at or behind it. More algorithm does not help when the features are redundant.
  • A different kind of model does help. Adding an online goal-based attack/defence rating (a running bivariate-Poisson, distinct from outcome-Elo) was the single biggest gain: test RPS 0.1686 → 0.1658 and log-loss 0.867 → 0.855. It captures scoring patterns Elo's win/loss view misses.
  • The ensemble is mostly those two. Optimised weights: GoalRating 0.50, Elo-Poisson 0.29, GradBoost 0.11, the rest tiny. The learners earn small weights as tie-breakers, not as the engine.
  • Calibration was already good — the optimal shrink-toward-base-rate was zero, and stacking slightly overfit. The honest best is the weighted blend.
Headline: from a 0.1686 Elo baseline to a 0.1657 ensemble — a ~1.7% RPS cut and a clearer 1.5% log-loss improvement. For international football (bookmakers sit around 0.18-0.19 RPS on broader samples) this is a strong, well-calibrated model, and the gains have plateaued: Elo + goals is the ceiling on the data we can reconstruct.

Into the 2026 forecast (v3)

We pushed the validated-best model (goal-rating, which natively produces scorelines) into the tournament simulator. It noticeably reshapes the title odds, and for the better:

v2 had Spain at a suspicious 32% (top Elo plus a soft group made the deterministic Elo-Poisson over-confident). The better-calibrated goal model spreads the probability across the genuine contenders — Argentina, Spain, Brazil, Colombia all 9-18% — which matches the lesson from the 2018/2022 backtest that even the favourite should rarely exceed the low-20s in a 48-team field.

Honest take & next ideas

  • The match-level gain is real but modest; the bigger win is a more credible title distribution and better probability calibration (log-loss).
  • The value/injury priors of v2 are not in v3 yet — a natural next step is to layer the injury penalty onto the goal-rating model too.
  • Further gains would need data we cannot reconstruct historically (lineups per match, xG). The pure-results ceiling is about here.
  • Tournament calibration confirmed: re-running the 2018/2022 backtest with the goal model puts France 2018 at #6 (4.8%) and Argentina 2022 at #2 (20.4%) — the same sensible placement of the eventual champions as the Elo engine, so v3's wider 2026 spread reflects real uncertainty, not a broken model.
