Intended use

  • Analysis, storytelling and education around the 2026 World Cup.
  • Producing calibrated probabilities for matches and tournament outcomes.
  • Out of scope: betting/financial decisions, and any use treating a probability as a certainty. A 30% favourite still loses most of the time.

Data

  • Training corpus: 40,994+ historical internationals (1872–2026) from the public results dataset; goals, competition, venue.
  • Current-state data: 48 squads with club stats, market values, recent form and head-to-head (collected 2026-05-26).
  • Strict time split — train < 2020-01-01, validate 2020-01-01–2023-01-01, test 2023-01-01–2026-05-26 (3,431 matches).

How it works

Two layers. A match model rates every team and turns any fixture into win/draw/loss probabilities and a scoreline; a Monte Carlo simulator plays the full 48-team tournament 20,000 times to turn those into title odds. The match model is an ensemble of an outcome Elo rating and an online goal-based attack/defence rating (a bivariate Poisson), with the goal model carrying most of the weight. The current default (v5) adds two small layers: an empirical confederation calibration derived from 2,057 cross-confederation matches, and a squad-depth prior from top-11 market value. Full walkthrough in the course; the bake-off is in model-bakeoff; the market check in model vs market.

Limitations & responsible use

  • Provisional squads. Collected before some final-26 deadlines; injuries (e.g. Xavi Simons) shift things.
  • Results-only signal. No per-match lineups or expected goals (xG); the model cannot see tactics, fatigue or in-game events.
  • Form & H2H were tested and excluded — they add nothing beyond Elo, which already encodes recent results.
  • Qualitative news signals (v4) are a labelled overlay, not the headline. An agent-team backtest found they don't improve held-out accuracy; they are kept evidence-weighted (injuries weighted most) and capped at ±0.06 so they cannot degrade the validated v3.
  • High variance is real. Even a strong favourite usually has under a 1-in-3 chance; the spread across contenders is a feature, not a hedge.
  • Fixtures vary by source in the per-team data; reconcile against the official schedule before using them.
