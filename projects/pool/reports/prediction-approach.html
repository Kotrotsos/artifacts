Predicting the 2026 World Cup

First-pass modelling approach · v1 · 2026-05-26 · companion to the data/ dataset

1. Objective

Two outputs:

  1. Match model — for any pairing (A vs B) at a given venue, output probabilities of A win / draw / B win, plus an expected scoreline.
  2. Tournament forecast — for each of the 48 teams, the probability of finishing top-2 in its group, reaching each knockout round, and winning the trophy.

Keep the two layers separate. The match model is the hard part; the tournament forecast is "run the match model 50,000 times and count."

3. The match model — three options, in order of effort

A. Elo baseline (start here)

One strength rating per team. Win probability is a logistic function of the rating gap plus a venue term:

P(A beats B) = 1 / (1 + 10^(-(EloA - EloB + H) / 400))

H is a home/neutral adjustment (hosts get a bump; everyone else is on neutral US/CA/MX soil). Trivial to implement, generalises to any pairing (critical for knockout sims), needs only historical results to seed. Ignores goals and squad detail.

B. Bivariate Poisson goals model (recommended target)

Model each team's goals as Poisson with rates from attack and defence strengths:

lambda_A = exp(mu + attackA - defenceB + home_adj)
lambda_B = exp(mu + attackB - defenceA)

Fit attack/defence per team (Dixon-Coles refinement: low-score correction + time-decay so recent matches count more). Gives a full scoreline distribution, so W/D/L, clean sheets and over/under all fall out. Interpretable and a genuinely strong football baseline.

C. Gradient-boosted classifier (the ML layer)

XGBoost/LightGBM predicting P(W/D/L) (or two goal regressors) from the engineered feature vector. Use it to beat the Poisson baseline and absorb features Elo/Poisson cannot (squad value, injuries, rest, travel).

Recommendation: ship Elo end-to-end first (fastest path to a real champion number), then upgrade the match model to Poisson + a GBM blending our squad/form/H2H features. Treat "DL or agents" as a later layer once the baseline is calibrated.

5. Training data (the biggest gap)

The current data/ is a snapshot of the present (who is in form, who is worth what). To train a model you need labelled history: thousands of past internationals with goals. The standard free option is the Kaggle "International football results 1872–present" dataset. It is used to seed Elo, fit Poisson attack/defence, train and cross-validate the GBM, and backtest on the 2018 and 2022 World Cups. Acquiring and cleaning it is the next real engineering task.

6. Tournament simulation (Monte Carlo)

One simulated tournament:

  1. Group stage — simulate all 72 group matches; rank each group by points then official tiebreakers (goal difference, goals for, head-to-head, then fair-play / lots). Top 2 per group plus the 8 best third-placed teams advance (32 total).
  2. Knockout — build the Round of 32 from the fixed advancement map; simulate single matches; on a draw, extra time then a penalty model (≈ coin flip, optionally tilted by shootout history). Advance through the Final.
  3. Record how far every team got.

Run ~50,000 times. A team's champion probability is the fraction of sims it won; same for "reached QF", "won group", etc.

7. Evaluation

  • Match level: Ranked Probability Score (the football standard for ordered W/D/L), plus log-loss and Brier; calibration plots.
  • Tournament level: compare simulated champion odds to the betting market (sanity check), and backtest the full pipeline on WC 2018 and 2022 using only pre-tournament data.
  • Always use a time-based train/test split (train pre-2022, test 2022+); never shuffle football matches.

8. Known modelling pitfalls

  • Draws are hard. Plain win-prob models under-predict draws; use an ordered or Poisson model with a real draw probability.
  • Friendlies lie. Weight competitive matches above friendlies; time-decay so 2026 form outweighs 2023.
  • Neutral venues. Almost every 2026 match is neutral except the three hosts; do not bake in generic home advantage.
  • Squad churn. Squads are provisional; injuries (e.g. Xavi Simons out) must flow into the availability feature.
  • H2H is sparse. Many pairings have few or zero meetings, so H2H is a small adjustment, never the backbone; ratings carry unseen pairings.
