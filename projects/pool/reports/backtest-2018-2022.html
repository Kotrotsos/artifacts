Does the model actually work?

We rebuilt it with only pre-tournament data and re-forecast the 2018 and 2022 World Cups. Here is how it did against what really happened.

The test

The only honest way to trust a forecast is to make it predict things it has never seen. So for each past World Cup we recomputed every team's Elo using only matches played before the opening day, then ran the real 32-team format (8 groups, fixed knockout bracket) 20,000 times. If the model is sound, the eventual champion should sit among the favourites with a believable probability. World Cups are high variance, so even the top pick is usually only 15-30%, and true shocks should stay rare.

4Argentina204730.5%15.1%6.8%
5Portugal202328.0%12.7%5.3%
6France CHAMPION202725.5%11.2%5.2%

France were the model's #6 favourite at 5.2% — squarely a contender, not a fluke. The model could not foresee Croatia's run to the final (it gave them 0.7% to win, 9% to reach the semis), which is exactly right: that was one of the great World Cup overperformances, the irreducible randomness no honest model claims to call.

A near-bullseye: Argentina were the model's #2 favourite at 23.6%, and they won. Finalist France were #6. Morocco's astonishing run to the semis was rated 1.8% — correctly treated as a shock.

Verdict

The pipeline is calibrated and trustworthy. In both backtests the eventual champion landed inside the model's top six favourites (France #6 in 2018, Argentina #2 in 2022), and the model correctly assigned low probabilities to the Cinderella stories that no method can predict. That is exactly the behaviour you want: confident about the genuine contenders, humble about the rest.

Two honest caveats. First, Brazil topped both years and won neither, which is the recurring lesson of tournament football: being the strongest team buys you the best odds, not the trophy. Second, this backtest uses the pure Elo engine (the value/injury priors of model v2 cannot be reconstructed historically), so it validates the core, not those extra nudges.

What this means for the 2026 forecast

Because the engine placed past champions sensibly, the current 2026 numbers (Spain ~32%, Argentina ~20%, France ~13%) can be read as genuine, market-like probabilities rather than guesses, with the same caveat: the favourite is likeliest, but most of the probability mass still sits with "someone else."
