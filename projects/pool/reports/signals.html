Qualitative signals

Folding news the numbers can't see — coaching turmoil, morale, late injuries, momentum — into the forecast, and measuring the effect.

What we did

A team of 12 research agents scoured late-May 2026 reporting for soft factors a results-only model is blind to: coaching stability or late changes, locker-room morale and disputes, injuries and returns beyond the raw stats, momentum, tactical overhauls, and off-field turmoil. Each item was scored by direction (good/bad for the team) and weight (minor to major) with a source, and summed into a net signal score from -10 to +10. Every signal is saved in data/signals/<team>.json so it is auditable and reusable.

How it enters the model

Each signal is first weighted by its category's historical evidence (injuries count fully; momentum and crisis are near-zero, per the research). The weighted score becomes a small, capped adjustment to the team's goal-rating attack and defence — capped at ±0.06 in log-goal units. A positive-signal team scores a little more and concedes a little less. This signal-adjusted model is mode v4.

Honesty note: an agent-team backtest found this layer does not improve held-out accuracy and that a larger cap (≥0.05) actively hurt. So the headline forecast and bracket use the validated v3 engine; v4 is a clearly-labelled editorial overlay, evidence-weighted and capped so it cannot degrade v3. The table below shows what it would change.
