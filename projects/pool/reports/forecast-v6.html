Overall assessment

The verifier reproduces the 30k claim within tight margins (max delta 0.55 pp across the 4 tournaments). No cherry-pick flag triggered: every per-tournament probability is within 3pp of the claim, and the actual champions land in the same neighbourhood under independent randomness. WC2022 swaps Argentina #1 / Brazil #2 (30k claim) for Argentina #2 / Brazil #1 (verifier 20k); both runs put them within 1pp of each other, so WC2022 is effectively a coin-flip in v6.1 between Argentina and Brazil. WC2018 France is rank 6 in both runs, which confirms that the WC2018 miss is a genuine model limitation and not a sampling artifact.

Verdict: PARTIAL. The forecast is not falsified by independent randomness, but the original "perfect last-3" target is not met by either run. The partial label reflects that the 30k canonical claim of rank 1 for WC2022 narrowly flips to rank 2 in the verifier, while all other per-WC rankings hold.

Methodology and signals

v6.1 is a signal-weighted Elo with Monte Carlo bracket simulation. The 2026 forecast is run at 100,000 sims, seed 42. Final locked parameters come from candidate s11 of sweep2 (24-candidate grid), validated at 30k. Refinement sweep3 ran 10 additional adaptive rounds aiming at the "perfect last-3" target but was not selected (see below).

Base Elo

Football Elo-style rating fitted from international results through 2026-05-26. The unweighted input. Adjusted Elo column shows the v6.1 input fed to the Poisson goal model.

Confederation calibration

Scale 60 Elo. Applies a per-confederation log-shift learned from cross-confederation results: UEFA +0.014, CONMEBOL +0.013, CAF +0.012, AFC -0.011, CONCACAF -0.018, OFC -0.064.

Host advantage

+70 Elo to USA, Canada, Mexico.

Squad strength (w_squad 0.8)

Per-player Elo from club performance, aggregated into best-XI averages and attack / midfield / defense block ratings. The squad strength score (z-scored) feeds directly into adjusted Elo.

Attack and defense block weights (0.6 each)

Independent contributions from attack-block average Elo and defense-block average Elo, scaled at 200. Counter-balances the all-player average.

Scorer-Elo (w_scorer 0.8)

Adjustment for goal-scoring talent at the team level, scaled at 0.5. Picks up signal that the overall squad average misses for top forwards.

WC bias (w_wc_bias 0.6)

Historical World Cup overperformance / underperformance relative to pre-tournament Elo. Symmetric in v6.1 (rewards consistent overperformers, penalizes chronic underperformers).

Recent-champion credit (w_recent_champ 0.0)

Tuned to zero in the locked s11 candidate. Refinement showed it overrewarded last cycle's finalists in 2026 without buying robustness on the last-3 backtest.

Defending-champion penalty (0)

Disabled in s11. Refinement rounds 6 to 10 tried up to -350 Elo to suppress past champions; trade-offs hurt the 2026 forecast more than they helped the backtest.

Poisson goals

Base 1.35 goals per side. Lambda derived from supremacy / 210 with a min lambda of 0.18 and a draw boost of 0.06.

Refinement history

v6.1 was selected after a 24-candidate sweep (sweep2) followed by 10 adaptive refinement rounds (sweep3) aimed at the "perfect last-3" target (WC2014, WC2018, WC2022 all rank 1). The refinement explored asymmetric WC bias, recent-champion credit, defending-champion penalties up to -350 Elo, and inverted confederation scaling. Best refinement round (r9) reached sum_rank_last3 = 7 by ranking WC2014 Germany #1 and WC2022 Argentina #1, but WC2018 France stayed at rank 5. The selection committee chose candidate s11 from the prior sweep over the refined r9 because the refinement results required parameter extremes (defending_champion_penalty = -300, w_scorer = 0, host_adv = 0) that overfit the 4-tournament window and degraded face validity of the 2026 forecast. s11 keeps the more conservative parameter set and reports sum_rank_last3 = 9 with log_score 7.21.

Honest reading: the refinement showed that getting France 2018 to rank 1 inside this signal stack is not possible without overfitting. France in 2018 entered with weaker pre-tournament fundamentals than Brazil, Germany, Spain, and Argentina on every signal v6.1 uses. The only signals that nudged France up were Euro 2016 runner-up credit and asymmetric WC bias, and they were not enough.
