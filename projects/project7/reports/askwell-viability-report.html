Viability and Business Model

Is AskWell worth building?

A read on the competitive landscape and a modeled business case, with the numbers stated so you can change the assumptions.

Research as of May 2026

Verdict

Viable, conditional on the wedge

Build AskWell as a requirements and stakeholder-discovery product, not a horizontal AI survey tool and not another customer-research platform. The core mechanic is proven and well funded, which de-risks whether it works but means the obvious lanes are already contested by capitalized incumbents. Requirements discovery is the one credible opening, with a named buyer and quantifiable ROI.

Competitive landscape

Three lanes, one place to play

Customer / UX research Red ocean, avoid

Crowded and very well capitalized. Listen Labs raised $27M (Sequoia) then a $69M Series B at a $500M valuation, with 1M+ interviews and 8-figure ARR. Outset (YC S23), Strella (Bessemer), User Intuition ($20/interview, 500+ teams), plus Conveo and Heymarvin. The thesis is validated, the positioning is taken.

Horizontal conversational forms Commoditizing, avoid

Squeezed from both ends. Typeform, Jotform, Qualtrics, SurveySparrow all added AI follow-ups; cheap challengers (Duonut, PingPolls, Formshare) crowd the bottom near $0.29/response. "Conversational survey" is now a feature, not a product.

Requirements & stakeholder discovery Open, the wedge

Under-served. What exists is meeting capture (Fireflies, Grain) and dev-tool plugins (Copilot4DevOps, Jama). Nobody owns "interview every stakeholder, return a requirements doc with a conflict map." AI elicitation captures ~37% more requirements and cuts requirements time ~50%, and 38% of projects stall in discovery. Named buyer, obvious ROI, no dominant incumbent.

The moat

The generic "AI conducts an interview" layer is now table stakes. Defensibility comes from requirements-specific synthesis (the conflict map, traceability), workflow integrations (Jira, Linear, Azure DevOps), and a conflict-detection model that improves with use. Own the workflow, not the chat.

Unit cost

What one interview costs us

A completed text interview is ~10 minutes, ~18 turns. With prompt caching, roughly 60K billed input tokens and 6K output, on a Sonnet-class model, plus a share of synthesis and infra.

Pricing

Hybrid: self-serve at the bottom, sales-led at the top

Anchor on value. A missed requirement caught early is worth weeks of rework, so the buyer compares AskWell to a research study or a rework disaster, never to a $29 form plan.

Overage $1.50/interview (~80% margin). Voice add-on +$1/interview. Annual billing: 2 months free. Worst-case margin (Team plan, full 500 interviews used): $150 COGS vs $399 = 62%; typical utilization pushes realized margin past 80%.

Principle

Charge for the synthesis, not the chat. Keep basic interviews cheap or free, gate the conflict map, traceability, and export. That is the value an incumbent will not prioritize.

Go-to-market

Beachhead: software consultancies and product agencies running client discovery; they feel the pain per engagement and bill it through. Expand to in-house PMs and business analysts. Land every demo on the conflict-map screen (Sales must-have vs Legal constraint), the one view that sells the product.
