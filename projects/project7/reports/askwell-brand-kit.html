What it is

An interviewer you can clone

AskWell is a conversational research platform. Instead of sending a static form and hoping for thoughtful answers, you build an AI assistant that interviews people on your behalf, the way you would build a custom GPT.

1. Build

Describe your goal in plain language. Set the tone. No prompt engineering.

2. Invite

Share a link or send it to a list. One person or ten thousand.

3. Converse

Each person has a natural, adaptive interview. The assistant digs in where it matters.

4. Return

Answers come back summarized into themes and structured fields, transcripts underneath.

The core insight

What makes an interview better than a form is not the human. It is the follow-up question, the adaptation, the listening. AI can now do that for every respondent at once. The scarce resource just stopped being scarce.

Pitch

The problem

Forms are cheap but shallow. People skim, skip, and abandon. The most valuable answer is always the one the form did not think to ask for.

Interviews are rich but do not scale. You cannot book thirty calls this week, and certainly not a thousand. So everyone settles.

Conversational AI is finally good enough to interview a stranger and stay on track. People already understand custom assistants. And the cost of a good conversation dropped to near zero, which flips the economics of research entirely.

Sample assistant spec

The Requirements Interviewer

A concrete, buildable example for the lead vertical. This is what a PM or business analyst would create, send to stakeholders, and get results from, the reference demo to build the product toward.

1. Setup (build like a custom GPT)

FieldExample value
Assistant nameRequirements Interviewer for the Customer Portal rebuild
GoalUnderstand what each stakeholder needs from the new portal, where their needs conflict, and what would make the project a failure in their eyes.
Who is interviewed8 to 12 stakeholders across Sales, Support, Product, Legal, and two enterprise customers.
Must-cover topicsJobs to be done, pain points, must-have vs. nice-to-have, success criteria, hard constraints, dealbreakers.
ToneProfessional, warm, concise. Senior-stakeholder appropriate.
Time budget8 to 12 minutes. Respect that they are busy.

2. Opening message

Hi, thanks for taking a few minutes. I am an assistant helping the team gather requirements for the Customer Portal rebuild. I will ask you a handful of questions about what you need from it, and I will dig in where it is useful. There are no wrong answers, I am here to capture your view accurately. This takes about 10 minutes and you can pause anytime. Ready to start?

Names the purpose, sets the time expectation, signals it will adapt, respects a senior person's time. The invite framing is part of the product, not an afterthought.

3. Interview flow (objectives, not a script)

  1. Role and stake.
    "What is your role, and how does the portal affect your work?" Tailor everything after this.
  2. Jobs to be done.
    "When you use the portal, what are you actually trying to get done?"
    Trigger: vague verbs (manage, handle) → "Walk me through the last time you did that, step by step."
  3. Pain points.
    "What about the current portal frustrates you most?"
    Trigger: complaint without impact → "How often, and what does it cost you when it happens?"
  4. Must-have vs. nice-to-have.
    "If we could only ship three things, what are your three? Which is a true dealbreaker?" The highest-value extraction.
  5. Success criteria.
    "Six months after launch, how would you judge whether it was worth it?"
    Trigger: soft answer → "Better how? What number or behavior would change?"
  6. Constraints and dealbreakers.
    "Any hard constraints I should flag, like compliance, security, integrations, or deadlines?"
  7. Open floor.
    "What have I not asked that I should have?" Often surfaces the most important requirement of all.

4. Follow-up behavior, the differentiator

  • Press on vagueness. Ask for a concrete recent example before moving on.
  • Quantify. Turn "slow" into seconds, "often" into a frequency, "important" into a ranking.
  • Reflect and confirm. "So the dealbreaker is X, and Y is nice-to-have. Did I get that right?"
  • Notice conflict signals. Flag likely clashes with other groups for the synthesis.
  • Know when to stop. A complete 10-minute interview beats an abandoned 25-minute one.
  • Stay neutral. Capture, do not steer. Do not lead the witness.

5. What comes back

Per stakeholder

Role and stake, jobs to be done, ranked pain points, must-haves, explicit dealbreakers, measurable success criteria, hard constraints, notable quotes, full transcript a click away.

Across all stakeholders

Consensus requirements, the conflict map, coverage gaps, aggregated risk flags, and a draft requirements list ready for the human to refine. This synthesis is the paid feature.
